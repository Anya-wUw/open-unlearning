\textbf{AdaPop} addresses the popularity gap in LLM unlearning by replacing local model confidence with an externally sourced popularity signal to calibrate per-fact gradient pressure, combined with a dual-ascent controller that automates forget-retain balancing. Across three model families and two real-world benchmarks, it produces deeper parametric erasure and stronger paraphrase generalisation than all stable baselines while preserving general capabilities. These gains require no per-fact manual tuning and no dataset-specific hyperparameter search.

The core finding is that local confidence is an insufficient proxy for memorisation depth. WGA suppresses surface-form recall but fails to generalise to paraphrases and adversarial reformulations. GA and GD collapse at all stable learning rates; NPO systematically under-erases popular facts. A global popularity signal narrows the popularity gap and makes erasure more robust. \textbf{AdaPop} is most effective for entity-centric factual QA where popularity proxies are available; Wikidata \texttt{pop\_sum} provides broad coverage, and LLM-estimated knowledgeability scores are a practical substitute.
