%% Sections 4–5: Experimental Setup and Results

We evaluate \textbf{AdaPop} against SOTA unlearning methods: GA~\cite{jang2022knowledge}, GD~\cite{liu2022continual}, NPO~\cite{zhang2024negative}, and WGA~\cite{wang2025rethinking} across three LLM families and two real-world knowledge benchmarks. All experiments use LoRA fine-tuning~\cite{hu2021lora} with rank~$r{=}32$, $\alpha{=}64$, and a fixed batch size of 1 with 32 gradient accumulation steps. Main results are reported at learning rate $10^{-4}$, which provides a stable operating point where all methods have non-zero gradients while avoiding the catastrophic collapse that uniformly afflicts unconstrained ascent methods at higher rates. A full sweep over five learning rates is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:lr_sensitivity}. AdaPop-specific parameters: initial retain coefficient $\alpha_0 = 0.5$, constraint threshold $\varepsilon = 0.1$, dual step size $\eta_\lambda = 0.1$, dual variable bound $\lambda_{\max} = 5.0$, popularity exponent coefficients $a{=}58.7$, $b{=}0.796$, clipped to $[0.05, 2.0]$. Per-step popularity modulation is disabled ($g=0$). Sensitivity of results to $\pm$20\% perturbations of $a$ and $b$ is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:coef_sensitivity}.

\subsection{Models}
We use Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct~\cite{grattafiori2024llama}, Gemma-7B-it~\cite{team2024gemma}, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct~\cite{qwen2025}. The 7--8B scale is standard in unlearning research~\cite{zhang2024negative, yao2024large, jin2024rwku}. These models share the transformer backbone but differ in pretraining and instruction-tuning regimes; per-model breakdowns appear in Appendix~\ref{app:model_specific}.

\subsection{Benchmarks}
We evaluate on two real-world knowledge datasets that exhibit fundamentally different popularity distributions (Figure~\ref{fig:benchmarks_pop_diverse}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DUET}~\cite{anna2026anatomy}: A city-centric factual QA benchmark with explicit popularity diversity. It covers a broad spectrum of Wikidata \texttt{pop\_sum} scores (range: 69--3{,}763; median: 1{,}089.5), enabling direct observation of the popularity gap between the rare and popular fact subsets. We use the combined rare-and-popular forget subset alongside a 500-sample fast retain set.
    \item \textbf{RWKU}~\cite{jin2024rwku}: A precision-focused benchmark testing targeted fact erasure while preserving semantically adjacent knowledge. Its popularity distribution is narrower and skewed to the lower end (range: 0--704; median: 130), making it suitable for evaluating whether the accuracy of forgetting degrades under lower popularity variance.
\end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{latex/img/pop_sum_diversity_comparison.png}
    \caption{Wikidata \texttt{pop\_sum} score distributions for the forget sets of DUET and RWKU. DUET spans nearly two orders of magnitude ($69$--$3{,}763$; median $1{,}090$), providing a direct test of popularity-sensitive methods; RWKU is narrower and skewed lower (median $130$).}
    \label{fig:benchmarks_pop_diverse}
\end{figure}

We exclude synthetic benchmarks such as TOFU~\cite{maini2024tofu}, whose narrow popularity distributions are insufficient to evaluate popularity-sensitive methods. DUET provides \texttt{pop\_sum} natively; for RWKU, we compute it by querying Wikidata for the inbound citelink counts of each fact's subject and object entities. Because widely-referenced entities appear proportionally across all major web-scale pretraining corpora, Wikidata citelink counts correlate with training-corpus frequency regardless of the specific pretraining regime~\cite{kandpal2023large}, making \texttt{pop\_sum} a corpus-agnostic proxy applicable across the three model families we evaluate. LLM-estimated knowledgeability scores are a practical alternative when Wikidata access is unavailable~\cite{anna2026anatomy, marina2025llm}.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We report six complementary metrics that jointly measure erasure depth and model integrity, grouped into output-level (§\ref{sec:main_results}) and internal representation metrics (§\ref{sec:internal}).

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ROUGE-L Recall}: token overlap between generated and gold answer. $\downarrow$ on forget, $\uparrow$ on retain.
    \item \textbf{Cosine Similarity (Cos Sim)}: embedding-space proximity to the gold label. Same directions.
    \item \textbf{$\Delta$ Log-Probability ($\Delta$LP)}: per-token log-likelihood shift from the base model. Large negative on forget; near-zero on retain.
    \item \textbf{$\Delta$ Rank}: change in ground-truth token rank in the predicted distribution. Large positive on forget; near-zero on retain.
    \item \textbf{Hidden-State Cosine Similarity (Hid.Cos)}: mean cosine of intermediate hidden states vs.\ the base model. Low on forget; high on retain.
    \item \textbf{KL Divergence (KL)}: KL of the output distribution from the base model. High on forget; low on retain.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Robustness Evaluation}
\label{sec:robustness}

ROUGE-L measures whether a model reproduces the gold answer verbatim, not whether the underlying fact has been erased. We evaluate two held-out subsets to probe generalisation of forgetting; results are in Table~\ref{tab:robustness}.

\paragraph{DUET paraphrase subsets.}
We report forget ROUGE-L on paraphrased versions of the DUET queries, split by popularity tier. Genuine knowledge removal requires low ROUGE-L on both original and paraphrase queries; high paraphrase ROUGE-L alongside low original ROUGE-L indicates surface-form suppression without disruption of the underlying factual encoding.

\paragraph{RWKU adversarial-robustness subset (Level-3).}
The RWKU benchmark includes an adversarial-attack evaluation; Level-3 queries apply nine prompt manipulation strategies to the forget set (including prefix injection, role-playing, reverse queries, and cross-lingual reformulations) to test whether a model can be induced to reveal knowledge it should have forgotten. Low ROUGE-L on Level-3 indicates that erasure resists these recovery attempts, reflecting genuine knowledge removal rather than surface suppression. A per-method analysis is in §\ref{sec:comparison}.

\subsection{Algorithm Comparison}
\label{sec:comparison}

\paragraph{GA and GD: collapse persists at reduced learning rate.}
GA induces complete collapse at $\text{lr}=10^{-4}$ across all three architectures (ROUGE-L $= 0.000$ on both splits); GD partially recovers retention (Llama/DUET R$=0.853$), but the retain quality remains substantially degraded. Neither method provides a safe unlearning regime.

\paragraph{NPO: insufficient forgetting at conservative learning rates.}
NPO preserves retain quality but consistently under-forgets on DUET (forget ROUGE-L $0.586$--$0.774$). Forget-split Hidden Cosine ($0.861$) and $\Delta$LP ($-42$) confirm minimal parametric change; Level-3 adversarial-attack ROUGE-L of $0.657$ (Llama) shows that knowledge remains recoverable under prompt manipulation.

\paragraph{WGA: strong surface forgetting, limited semantic depth.}
WGA achieves the best surface-level forgetting at $\text{lr}=10^{-4}$ (DUET forget ROUGE-L $0.036$--$0.057$; retain $\geq 0.993$). However, $\Delta$Rank on DUET is negative ($-3{,}714$): the ground-truth token's rank \emph{improves} after unlearning, indicating that log-probability-weighted ascent redistributes probability mass rather than suppressing the factual trace. Paraphrase evaluation confirms shallow erasure: popular-paraphrase forget ROUGE-L reaches $0.194$ (Qwen) and $0.096$ (Gemma), vs.\ \textbf{AdaPop}'s $0.055$ and $0.028$. Level-3 adversarial-attack ROUGE-L ($0.211$--$0.396$) likewise exceeds \textbf{AdaPop}'s ($0.144$--$0.262$), showing that forgotten knowledge remains partially recoverable under prompt manipulation.

\paragraph{\textbf{AdaPop}: deeper semantic erasure with paraphrase generalisation.}
On DUET, \textbf{AdaPop}'s forget ROUGE-L ($0.027$--$0.051$) is comparable to WGA ($0.036$--$0.057$), but the gap opens on deeper metrics: forget Cosine Similarity is lower ($0.206$--$0.456$ vs.\ $0.237$--$0.477$), and $\Delta$Rank is 2--3 orders of magnitude larger ($+53{,}760$ vs.\ $-3{,}714$). The retain cost is real: WGA retains higher ROUGE-L ($0.993$--$0.997$ vs.\ $0.942$--$0.966$), but popular-paraphrase forget ROUGE-L stays below $0.060$ across all models (vs.\ WGA's $0.067$--$0.194$), confirming genuine factual erasure. On RWKU, \textbf{AdaPop} achieves lower forget ROUGE-L across all models (Llama: $0.044$ vs.\ $0.096$; Qwen: $0.015$ vs.\ $0.057$) at comparable retain ROUGE-L, and Level-3 adversarial-attack ROUGE-L of $0.144$--$0.262$ confirms that erasure resists prompt-based recovery. Per-model breakdowns, additional baselines (UNDIAL, RMU, PDU), and epoch dynamics are presented in Appendices~\ref{app:model_specific}--\ref{app:epoch_dynamics}.

\paragraph{Summary.}
GA/GD collapse or severely damage the retain quality at all stable learning rates. NPO under-erases well-memorised facts; WGA achieves surface forgetting but fails paraphrase generalisation. \textbf{AdaPop} occupies a better tradeoff: deeper semantic erasure ($\Delta$Rank $+53{,}760$; popular-paraphrase ROUGE-L $\leq 0.060$) at modest retain cost on DUET, and a cleaner forget-retain balance on RWKU. An ablation study confirms the dual-ascent controller and popularity exponent are complementary (cf. Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}). The higher retain ROUGE-L above Orig on Gemma and Qwen on RWKU reflects supervised descent on retain facts, not spurious overfitting, as MMLU and HellaSwag (cf. Appendix~\ref{app:lm_eval}) confirm no general capability degradation.
