\section{Derivation of AdaPop Popularity Parameters}
\label{app:derivation}

The power-law relationship $\beta_i = a \cdot s_i^{-b}$ is designed to map the wide dynamic range of popularity scores to a stable interval suitable for gradient updates. Since the gap between rare and popular entities typically spans several orders of magnitude in most popularity proxy metrics, the coefficients $a$ and $b$ must be rigorously tuned to ensure that the unlearning signal is appropriately scaled to the target facts' parametric encoding depth.

\paragraph{Theoretical Anchors and Boundary Conditions.}
Because \texttt{stopgrad} is applied to $w_{i,t} = p_{i,t}^{\beta_i}$, the gradient of the forget loss flows only through the NLL term $\ell_{i,t}$. The effective per-token gradient weight is therefore proportional to $p_{i,t}^{\beta_i - 1}$. The sign and magnitude of $\beta_i - 1$ determine the qualitative unlearning regime:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\beta_i > 1$: the effective weight $p_{i,t}^{\beta_i - 1}$ is an increasing function of $p_{i,t}$. As unlearning proceeds and token confidence drops, the update weight decreases proportionally. This creates a \emph{self-limiting} mechanism: the gradient naturally attenuates once a token's probability has fallen, preventing continued erasure of already-forgotten content.
    \item $\beta_i = 1$: the effective weight is constant ($p_{i,t}^0 = 1$), independent of confidence. Every token contributes uniformly, equivalent to standard weighted gradient ascent with fixed weights.
    \item $\beta_i < 1$: the effective weight $p_{i,t}^{\beta_i - 1}$ increases as $p_{i,t}$ decreases, approaching the inverse-confidence behaviour of vanilla Gradient Ascent as $\beta_i \to 0$. This amplifies the update on tokens that remain well-memorised despite ongoing unlearning, sustaining pressure throughout training.
\end{itemize}

Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025rethinking} analyse this gradient structure and show that $\beta = 1$ marks the boundary where the G-effect analysis transitions from harmful over-erasure dynamics (below 1, resembling GA) to a regime where the retain G-effect magnitude outweighs the unlearning G-effect, making integrity damage smaller than the unlearning benefit.

We use this structure to derive the two anchor points. Let $s_r$ be a representative popularity score for a rare fact and $s_p$ for a popular fact. The boundary conditions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rare facts}: $\beta(s_r) = 1.5$. A value comfortably above the $\beta = 1$ boundary places rare facts in the self-limiting regime: as a lightly-memorised fact is erased, the per-token weight attenuates automatically, preventing collateral over-erasure. The margin above 1 ensures the mechanism is meaningfully engaged even for facts that are only moderately memorised.
    \item \textbf{Popular facts}: $\beta(s_p) = 0.1$. A value near the $\beta = 0$ (GA) limit applies near-maximal gradient pressure on deeply memorised content: tokens that resist erasure receive large effective weights, sustaining the unlearning signal throughout training. The retain safeguard at this aggressive setting is provided by the dual-ascent controller rather than by $\beta_p$ itself.
\end{enumerate}

Given the power-law formulation $\beta(s) = a \cdot s^{-b}$, substituting the anchor points yields a system of equations:
\begin{align}
    \beta(s_r) &= a \cdot s_r^{-b} = 1.5 \\
    \beta(s_p) &= a \cdot s_p^{-b} = 0.1
\end{align}

\paragraph{Parameter Derivation.}
To isolate the exponent $b$, we divide the first equation by the second:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\beta(s_r)}{\beta(s_p)} = \left(\frac{s_r}{s_p}\right)^{-b} = \frac{1.5}{0.1} = 15
\end{equation}

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides gives $b$:
\begin{equation}
    b = \frac{\ln(15)}{\ln(s_p / s_r)}
\end{equation}

Given $b$, the scaling constant $a$ follows from the rare anchor:
\begin{equation}
    a = 1.5 \cdot s_r^b
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Empirical Calibration via Wikidata.}
When the popularity proxy labeling is based on Wikidata metrics (such as the number of site links or \texttt{pop\_sum}), we analyze the dataset distribution to extract representative anchor values. In our standard experimental setup, we observe that typical rare facts cluster around $s_r \approx 100$, while typical popular facts cluster around $s_p \approx 3000$.

Substituting these representative values into our derived formulas yields the numerical coefficients:
\begin{align}
    b &= \frac{\ln(15)}{\ln(3000 / 100)} = \frac{\ln(15)}{\ln(30)} \approx 0.796 \\
    a &= 1.5 \cdot 100^{0.796} \approx 58.7
\end{align}

To guarantee training stability and prevent gradient explosion under extreme outlier scores, the final exponent is clipped:
\begin{equation}
    \beta_i = \mathrm{clip}\left(58.7 \cdot s_i^{-0.796}, \beta_{\min}, \beta_{\max}\right)
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{\min} = 0.05$ and $\beta_{\max} = 2.0$. Any alternative popularity proxy (e.g., Wikidata pagerank, LLM-estimated knowledgeability, or co-occurrence counts in a large text corpus) can be used by substituting its own representative anchor values $s_r$ and $s_p$ into the same system of equations.

\paragraph{Robustness to Proxy Choice and the Three-Tier Requirement.}
The derivation depends only on two representative scores $s_r$ and $s_p$, not on exact distributional knowledge of the proxy. Because $b = \ln(15)/\ln(s_p/s_r)$ depends only on the \emph{ratio} $s_p/s_r$, moderate errors in the absolute anchor values shift $b$ by at most a few tenths and $a$ proportionally; the $\pm$20\% perturbation experiment in Appendix~\ref{app:coef_sensitivity} confirms this robustness within that range. The practical requirement is therefore that the proxy places rare and popular facts at least one order of magnitude apart, so that $\ln(s_p/s_r)$ is large enough to produce a meaningful $b$. Any proxy satisfying this condition yields coefficients in the correct qualitative regime: rare facts receive $\beta > 1$ (self-limiting), popular facts receive $\beta < 1$ (pressure-sustaining), and \emph{mid-popularity} facts (those between the two anchors) fall near $\beta \approx 1$, where the self-limiting and pressure-sustaining dynamics transition smoothly.

This three-tier structure is the critical requirement, not the specific proxy values. A proxy that collapses the mid-popularity tier fails even if it correctly separates the extremes. As a concrete example: suppose a proxy assigns $s_i \in \{100, 101, 3000\}$, where 100 is rare and both 101 and 3000 are labelled ``popular'' by a coarse threshold. The power law then maps $s_i = 100$ to $\beta \approx 1.5$ and $s_i = 101$ to $\beta \approx 1.49$, making moderately known facts indistinguishable from rare ones: they enter the self-limiting regime and are under-erased. The true popular facts at $s_i = 3000$ receive $\beta \approx 0.1$ as intended, but the mid-popularity interval is left without appropriate gradient pressure. Wikidata \texttt{pop\_sum} satisfies this requirement: it is a continuous score spanning three or more decades for typical knowledge benchmarks. In the DUET forget set, scores range from $69$ to $3{,}763$ with median $1{,}090$, placing a substantial fraction of facts in the mid-popularity interval $[150, 1500]$ where $\beta$ transitions through the $\beta = 1$ boundary. A proxy is adequate if it exhibits a comparable dynamic range and monotone correlation with pretraining corpus frequency; the anchor values $s_r \approx 100$ and $s_p \approx 3000$ used here are deliberately round numbers that represent order-of-magnitude positions, not precise distributional statistics.





\section{AdaPop Coefficient Sensitivity}
\label{app:coef_sensitivity}

The popularity exponent $\beta_i = \mathrm{clip}(a \cdot s_i^{-b},\, 0.05,\, 2.0)$ depends on two scalar coefficients derived analytically from boundary conditions on the DUET dataset (Appendix~\ref{app:derivation}: $a{=}58.7$, $b{=}0.796$). We assess how sensitive main-paper results are to errors in these estimates by perturbing each coefficient independently by $\pm$20\%, yielding four off-baseline configurations. All runs use Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on DUET at $\text{lr}=10^{-4}$; results are in Table~\ref{tab:coef_sensitivity}.

Forget ROUGE-L changes by at most 0.021 across all four perturbations; the ranking of AdaPop relative to WGA and NPO is preserved in every configuration. The retain metric is more sensitive: the $(-a,+b)$ configuration reduces retain by 0.041, whereas configurations that lower $b$ (a flatter beta schedule) improve retain by up to 0.033. The mechanism follows directly: lowering $b$ increases $\beta_i$ for all facts simultaneously, shifting them toward the self-limiting regime ($\beta > 1$) and concentrating gradient mass on the highest-confidence tokens; this produces less disruptive per-step updates and a lower retain penalty. Raising $b$ has the opposite effect, pushing popular facts deeper into the near-GA regime and amplifying gradient spread, which stresses the dual-ascent controller more. The $(-a,+b)$ case is the worst on retain but also achieves the strongest forgetting (0.024), illustrating the standard forget-retain tradeoff rather than any instability of the method. Overall, the analytically derived $a{=}58.7$, $b{=}0.796$ lies in a flat region of the retain surface; $\pm$20\% perturbations do not change the qualitative conclusions of the main paper.

\section{Comparison with UNDIAL, RMU, and PDU}
\label{app:additional_baselines}

We evaluate three methods not included in the main comparison: UNDIAL~\cite{dong2025undial}, Representation Misdirection for Unlearning (RMU)~\cite{li2024representation}, and Primal-Dual Unlearning (PDU) \cite{entesariconstrained}. These methods were excluded from the main tables because they represent distinct design paradigms whose assumptions do not directly target entity-level factual memorisation: UNDIAL applies a self-distillation penalty to suppress distributional knowledge, RMU redirects hidden-state representations toward a target distribution, and PDU combines a dual-ascent retain controller with a uniform gradient ascent objective. Including them here contextualises the contribution of popularity-aware gradient weighting relative to these alternative paradigms. All methods use identical LoRA configuration ($r{=}32$, $\alpha{=}64$, lr $= 10^{-4}$, 5 epochs) and are evaluated on the same forget and retain splits as the main tables. Tables~\ref{tab:baselines-llama}--\ref{tab:baselines-gemma} report ROUGE-L Recall alongside the \textbf{Orig} and \textbf{AdaPop} rows from Tables~\ref{tab:rouge-llama}--\ref{tab:rouge-gemma} for reference.

\paragraph{UNDIAL and RMU do not unlearn on DUET.}
Both methods produce forget ROUGE-L between $0.734$ and $0.884$ across all three models on DUET, barely below the Orig baseline ($0.892$--$0.939$). RMU was designed for skill-based hazard-knowledge unlearning (e.g., WMDP), where the target is a distributional pattern rather than a specific memorised fact. UNDIAL targets general LLM unlearning but relies on self-distillation and decoupled gradient signals that do not directly address the parametric depth at which entity-level facts are stored. Consequently, UNDIAL's self-distillation penalty and RMU's representation misdirection, as applied via LoRA, are insufficient to overcome the parametric encoding depth of the well-memorised popular city facts that dominate the DUET forget set. The high retain ROUGE-L ($\geq 0.980$ for UNDIAL on Llama) is a direct consequence of negligible forgetting rather than effective knowledge preservation. On RWKU, where the Orig forget baseline is lower ($0.471$--$0.755$), both methods achieve more meaningful erasure; yet their forget ROUGE-L remains substantially above \textbf{AdaPop} (e.g., Llama: UNDIAL $0.568$, RMU $0.801$ vs.\ \textbf{AdaPop} $0.078$).

\paragraph{PDU achieves intermediate forgetting at a retain cost.}
PDU's dual-ascent retain controller reduces forget ROUGE-L to $0.476$--$0.677$ on DUET and $0.201$--$0.455$ on RWKU, outperforming UNDIAL and RMU in erasure depth. However, this comes at a measurable retain penalty: retain ROUGE-L drops to $0.869$ (Qwen/DUET) and $0.710$ (Qwen/RWKU), below the $\geq 0.855$ floor maintained by \textbf{AdaPop} across all settings. Without popularity-calibrated exponent scaling, the dual controller applies an insufficiently targeted forgetting signal, requiring stronger overall gradient steps that incidentally degrade retain-split representations.

\paragraph{AdaPop dominates on erasure depth across all models.}
\textbf{AdaPop} achieves the lowest forget ROUGE-L among all stable methods on both benchmarks and all three architectures by a large margin (e.g., Llama/DUET: $0.045$ vs.\ PDU $0.476$; Gemma/RWKU: $0.021$ vs.\ PDU $0.404$). Retain quality is simultaneously stronger: \textbf{AdaPop} exceeds $0.855$ on all model-benchmark combinations, while PDU drops to $0.710$ on Qwen/RWKU. These results isolate popularity-calibrated exponent scaling as the critical factor; the dual-ascent retain controller alone (as in PDU) is necessary but not sufficient for deep, generalised forgetting.

Taken together, these three methods illustrate the limits of each design paradigm in isolation: distributional suppression (UNDIAL, RMU) is ineffective for entity-level factual erasure; constrained optimisation without popularity weighting (PDU) improves over distributional approaches but cannot match the erasure depth achieved by calibrating gradient pressure to memorisation depth. Only the combination of popularity-aware exponents and a dynamic retain controller achieves both deep forgetting and stable retention across all model families and benchmarks tested.

\section{General Capability Preservation: Extended Discussion}
\label{app:lm_eval}

This section examines whether the unlearning process affects general language understanding, as measured by MMLU (multi-domain factual knowledge) and HellaSwag (commonsense sentence completion). These two benchmarks test complementary aspects of capability: MMLU reflects static factual recall across 57 domains, while HellaSwag requires consistent reasoning over multi-token context windows. Using both allows us to detect degradation that might affect fluency or reasoning without necessarily reducing knowledge retrieval.

Table~\ref{tab:lm_eval} (main paper, §\ref{sec:lm_eval}) reports results for all methods at $\text{lr}=10^{-4}$, averaged across DUET and RWKU checkpoints.

\paragraph{GA: complete capability collapse.}
GA destroys both MMLU and HellaSwag across all three model families (MMLU $0.23$--$0.25$ vs.\ Orig $0.47$--$0.71$). This is consistent with the unbounded ascent signal observed in ROUGE-L: the model loses linguistic coherence within epoch~1 and no capability survives.

\paragraph{GD: MMLU preserved, HellaSwag degraded.}
GD shows a distinctive asymmetry: MMLU stays close to Orig (within $0.01$ for Llama and Qwen; equal for Gemma), yet HellaSwag drops substantially (Llama: $0.73 \to 0.59$; Gemma: $0.64 \to 0.37$). MMLU multiple-choice items depend on independent factual recall, which the retain objective directly reinforces. HellaSwag sentence completion requires sustained coherence over context, which is more sensitive to the gradient disruption caused by insufficient retain regularisation. The gap between MMLU and HellaSwag preservation in GD is therefore consistent with a retain coefficient that is adequate to protect factual knowledge but insufficient to maintain reasoning fluency.

\paragraph{NPO, WGA, AdaPop: general capabilities preserved.}
NPO, WGA, and \textbf{AdaPop} all remain within $0.05$ MMLU of Orig across all three architectures. WGA and \textbf{AdaPop} both apply supervised descent on the retain split, pushing the model to lower loss on retained facts throughout training; because the retain split is drawn from the same broad knowledge distribution evaluated by MMLU and HellaSwag, continued descent can marginally sharpen calibration on held-out benchmark items beyond the Orig checkpoint. This explains why WGA shows HellaSwag at or above Orig across all three models, and why \textbf{AdaPop} matches or exceeds Orig on Llama and Qwen, with a small drop on Gemma ($0.62$ vs.\ $0.64$). By contrast, NPO (which uses KL regularisation on the retain split rather than direct descent) stays at or slightly below Orig across all three models. The hidden-state cosine similarity for \textbf{AdaPop} on the retain split is comparable to WGA and NPO (Table~\ref{tab:internal_agg}), confirming that the slight capability gain reflects output calibration rather than representational drift. The pattern is consistent across all three model families, ruling out architecture-specific artefacts.

Collectively, these results confirm that targeted factual unlearning and general capability preservation are orthogonal concerns when retain regularisation is applied consistently. The primary risk is over-aggressive forgetting (GA, and GD at inadequate retain weighting), not popularity-aware gradient calibration. The challenge in LLM unlearning lies in achieving deep erasure of specific facts, not in avoiding collateral capability damage.

\section{Design Motivation: Gradient-Based Unlearning and the Retain Controller}
\label{app:design_motivation}

Gradient Ascent (GA) directly maximises the negative log-likelihood on the forget set, but its instability is well-documented: the unbounded ascent signal propagates through shared parameters and frequently destroys linguistic coherence~\cite{zhang2024negative, yao2024large}. GD~\cite{liu2022continual} mitigates this with a retain loss, but treats all forget tokens uniformly. WGA~\cite{wang2025rethinking} improves upon GD by weighting each token's gradient by the model's current confidence $p_{i,t}$: tokens the model recalls confidently receive a stronger unlearning signal, reducing unnecessary gradient noise on facts that are already near-forgotten. Our results (Tables~\ref{tab:rouge-llama}--\ref{tab:rouge-gemma}) confirm that WGA substantially outperforms GA and GD on retain quality.

AdaPop extends WGA by replacing the confidence-based weight with a per-fact exponent $\beta_i$ derived from an external popularity score. The key distinction is that confidence is a \emph{local} signal: it reflects how well the model currently recalls a given token, which changes throughout training and does not capture how deeply a fact is encoded across layers and attention heads. Popularity is a \emph{global} signal: it reflects pretraining corpus frequency, which is fixed and correlates with parametric memorisation depth~\cite{hartmann2024undesirable, kandpal2023large}. For popular facts, $\beta_i < 1$ spreads the ascent more broadly across token positions; for rare facts, $\beta_i > 1$ concentrates the update on the highest-confidence tokens, limiting collateral drift. The paraphrase evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:robustness}) provides direct evidence for this: WGA's popular-paraphrase forget ROUGE-L ($0.067$--$0.194$) substantially exceeds \textbf{AdaPop}'s ($0.028$--$0.055$), indicating that confidence-based weighting suppresses surface tokens without disrupting the underlying encoding.

\subsection{Dynamic Retain Coefficient}

In all gradient ascent variants, the retain coefficient $\alpha$ governs the forget-retain tradeoff. GD and WGA use a fixed $\alpha$ that must be tuned per dataset and model family: too small, and retain quality degrades; too large, and well-memorised popular facts survive erasure. This sensitivity is well-recognised in the literature~\cite{bu2025unlearning}.

The principled solution is to treat $\alpha$ as a Lagrange multiplier enforcing the constraint $\delta_k \le \varepsilon$ on retain-loss drift. Entesari et al.~\cite{entesariconstrained} formalise exactly this approach for LLM unlearning, demonstrating at NeurIPS 2025 that a primal-dual controller is more stable than fixed-penalty objectives. AdaPop adopts the same constrained formulation with two practical differences: (1) the dual variable $\lambda$ is updated once per epoch rather than per step, which reduces oscillation when individual-batch retain losses fluctuate; (2) the controller is applied to the popularity-weighted forget objective $L_f$ rather than the entropic forget objective in~\cite{entesariconstrained}.

We do not claim formal convergence guarantees for the non-convex training setting: dual ascent on non-convex problems can exhibit limit cycles and lacks the theoretical guarantees available for convex programs. Empirically, the controller maintains a retain loss within $\varepsilon{=}0.1$ of the epoch-1 reference across all three model families and both benchmarks, and the retain-split $\Delta$LP and Hid.Cos metrics for \textbf{AdaPop} are indistinguishable from WGA (Table~\ref{tab:internal_agg}), confirming that no unintended retain-set degradation occurs under the dynamic controller.

In summary, AdaPop's two components address two orthogonal failure modes: the popularity exponent corrects insufficient gradient pressure on deeply memorised facts (evidenced by the paraphrase evaluation in Table~\ref{tab:robustness} and the popularity-tier split in Figure~\ref{fig:lr_duet_split}), while the dual-ascent controller prevents retain degradation under increasing forget pressure (evidenced by the component ablation in Appendix~\ref{app:ablation} and the learning-rate sweep in Appendix~\ref{app:lr_sensitivity}). The interaction between these components is confirmed by the ablation result that neither component alone achieves the forget-retain balance of the full system.

\section{Component Ablation: Dual-Ascent Controller vs.\ Popularity Exponent}
\label{app:ablation}

We ablate the two AdaPop components independently by varying whether the retain coefficient $\alpha$ is fixed or controlled by the dual-ascent update, and whether the per-fact exponent $\beta_i$ is fixed at a small constant or set by the popularity power-law. This gives four variants:

The dual-ascent controller addresses retain stability under increasing forget pressure: without it, a fixed retain penalty is overwhelmed as the forget gradient grows at higher learning rates or for more memorised facts. The popularity exponent addresses the distribution of gradient signal across token positions: without it, all facts receive identical gradient weighting regardless of how deeply they are encoded in model parameters. Because these two failure modes are orthogonal, the ablation can isolate their contributions independently, and both components are expected to be necessary for the full AdaPop performance.

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\alpha{=}0.5,\,\beta{=}0.1$: fixed retain coefficient, fixed near-uniform exponent.
    \item $\alpha{=}0.5,\,\beta{=}\text{dyn}$: fixed retain coefficient, popularity-based $\beta_i$.
    \item $\alpha{=}\text{dyn},\,\beta{=}0.1$: dual-ascent controller, fixed near-uniform exponent.
    \item $\alpha{=}\text{dyn},\,\beta{=}\text{dyn}$: full AdaPop.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:ablation} reports forget paraphrase ROUGE-L ($\downarrow$) and retain ROUGE-L ($\uparrow$) by popularity tier across learning rates on DUET. The rare-fact plots span $\text{lr} \in [2\times10^{-5},\,8\times10^{-4}]$; the popular-fact plots span $\text{lr} \in [2\times10^{-4},\,8\times10^{-3}]$. The order-of-magnitude difference in the learning rate required to achieve comparable forgetting between the two tiers is itself direct evidence of the popularity gap: popular facts are substantially more resistant to gradient-based erasure.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{latex/img/coeffitient_oblation.jpg}
    \caption{Component ablation on DUET. \textit{Top row}: rare-fact paraphrase forget ROUGE-L ($\downarrow$) and retain ROUGE-L ($\uparrow$) over $\text{lr} \in [2\times10^{-5}, 8\times10^{-4}]$. \textit{Bottom row}: popular-fact paraphrase forget ROUGE-L ($\downarrow$) and retain ROUGE-L ($\uparrow$) over $\text{lr} \in [2\times10^{-4}, 8\times10^{-3}]$.}
    \label{fig:ablation}

\paragraph{Dual-ascent controller: primary driver of retain stability.}
The clearest signal across all four panels is the benefit of dynamic $\alpha$. Without the controller ($\alpha{=}0.5$), retain ROUGE-L on rare facts drops to $0.769$ at $\text{lr}{=}2\times10^{-4}$, while both dyn-$\alpha$ variants maintain $\geq 0.927$. At higher learning rates on popular facts, fixed-$\alpha$ variants lose retain ROUGE-L substantially ($\leq 0.634$ at $\text{lr}{=}5\times10^{-3}$), while the controller keeps retain above $0.72$. The controller addresses a failure mode independent of popularity weighting: without it, the forget gradient eventually overwhelms the fixed retain penalty, regardless of how the gradient is distributed across tokens.

\paragraph{Popularity exponent: improves the forget-retain balance at the frontier.}
At the learning rates where both dyn-$\alpha$ variants achieve comparable forget ROUGE-L ($\approx 0.02$ on popular facts), full AdaPop ($\alpha{=}\text{dyn},\,\beta{=}\text{dyn}$) retains $0.724$ versus $0.683$ for the controller-only variant ($\alpha{=}\text{dyn},\,\beta{=}0.1$). The popularity exponent assigns $\beta_i{<}1$ to popular facts, distributing the ascent signal more broadly across token positions and making forgetting more targeted; this allows the controller to apply less retain pressure for the same forgetting effect, improving the tradeoff at the frontier.

\paragraph{Fixed $\alpha$ with dynamic $\beta_i$ is the worst configuration.}
$\alpha{=}0.5,\,\beta{=}\text{dyn}$ achieves the highest popular-paraphrase forget ROUGE-L across the entire learning rate sweep, reaching $0.814$ at $\text{lr}{=}2\times10^{-3}$ and barely decreasing from the Orig baseline in that range. The mechanism is: for popular facts, $\beta_i{<}1$ distributes gradient mass broadly across tokens, producing weak per-token update signals. Without the dual-ascent controller to allow the forget loss to strengthen when retain is stable, these weak signals are insufficient to overcome the memorisation depth of popular facts. This result confirms that the two components are complementary: the popularity exponent calibrates the direction of the gradient, while the controller determines how aggressively the forget signal is allowed to act.

Overall, the ablation establishes that both components are individually necessary and jointly sufficient: the dual-ascent controller alone achieves retain stability but cannot reach deep forgetting of popular facts, while the popularity exponent alone improves gradient targeting but collapses without the controller to sustain adequate forget pressure. The full AdaPop configuration is the only variant that achieves simultaneously low forget ROUGE-L and high retain ROUGE-L across the tested learning rate range.

The answer determines whether a method has a stable operating region that practitioners can exploit, or whether it requires exact hyperparameter tuning to avoid either inadequate erasure or retain collapse.

\paragraph{GA and GD: no stable operating region.}
GA collapses on DUET at every learning rate tested, driving both forget and retain ROUGE-L to zero across all three model families (Figure~\ref{fig:lr_duet}, dark blue). GD fares marginally better at $10^{-6}$ but retain quality remains substantially degraded at any learning rate that achieves meaningful forgetting. On RWKU, the same pattern holds: both methods are unusable at learning rates above $10^{-6}$, confirming that neither has a viable operating regime on either benchmark.

\paragraph{NPO: stable but learning-rate-insensitive on DUET.}
NPO (orange) is the most stable method in terms of retain quality: it stays near-perfect across all learning rates on both benchmarks. On DUET, however, forget ROUGE-L barely moves as learning rate increases; the curves are nearly flat, confirming that the preference-alignment signal cannot overcome high parametric memorisation regardless of gradient magnitude. On RWKU, where baseline memorisation is lower, NPO achieves reasonable forgetting at higher learning rates, but the Cosine Similarity (dashed) remains the highest among non-collapsing methods, indicating shallow representational change.

\paragraph{WGA: model-dependent stability.}
WGA achieves competitive forgetting at $\text{lr}=10^{-4}$ but its behaviour at higher learning rates is model-dependent. On Llama, it remains stable across all tested rates. On Qwen and Gemma, the retain quality degrades visibly at $\text{lr} \geq 5{\times}10^{-5}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:lr_duet}, light blue retain panel), and Cosine Similarity on the forget split does not decrease proportionally to ROUGE-L, indicating surface suppression without deeper representational change. The absence of an automatic retain controller means that the optimal learning rate for WGA must be found by grid search for each model and benchmark.

\paragraph{AdaPop: monotonically improving forget with controlled retain.}
AdaPop (red) is the only method for which forget quality improves monotonically with learning rate on both benchmarks and all models, while retain quality remains controlled throughout. The dual-ascent controller compensates for the stronger forget signal at higher learning rates by increasing the retain coefficient, preventing the retain degradation observed in WGA and GD. On RWKU, retain ROUGE-L rises with learning rate rather than falling, reflecting the supervised retain-loss minimisation discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:lm_eval}. Cosine Similarity on the forget split decreases in parallel with ROUGE-L across all models and learning rates, confirming that the forgetting is representational rather than surface-level.

\paragraph{Popularity-tier asymmetry on DUET (Figure~\ref{fig:lr_duet_split}).}
The split plot shows that rare and popular facts occupy qualitatively different difficulty regimes. For Llama, rare facts reach near-zero forget ROUGE-L and Cosine Similarity at $\text{lr} \approx 5{\times}10^{-5}$; popular facts require $\text{lr} \approx 10^{-4}$ to achieve comparable values, and even then WGA's Cosine Similarity (dashed, popular forget panel) remains higher than AdaPop's. NPO fails entirely on the popular tier: its forget curves are flat across the full learning rate range, matching the Orig baseline. These patterns confirm that the popularity gap is an intrinsic property of how facts are encoded, not an artefact of any single learning rate choice.

\paragraph{Stability to popularity distribution diversity.}
Comparing Figures~\ref{fig:lr_duet} and~\ref{fig:lr_rwku} with Figure~\ref{fig:lr_duet_split} reveals two additional stability properties of \textbf{AdaPop}. First, it is robust to the diversity of the popularity distribution: DUET spans nearly two orders of magnitude in \texttt{pop\_sum} (range $69$--$3{,}763$) while RWKU is narrower and lower (range $0$--$704$), yet \textbf{AdaPop} achieves controlled forgetting and stable retention on both. Methods without a popularity-aware signal, particularly WGA, are more sensitive to this shift: retain quality on RWKU is higher for WGA because its confidence-based weighting underestimates the forget signal needed for lower-memorisation facts, whereas \textbf{AdaPop}'s per-fact exponent adjusts to the local distribution automatically.

\paragraph{Stability to batch composition.}
\textbf{AdaPop} is also stable to variation in batch composition across popularity tiers. The merged results (Figure~\ref{fig:lr_duet}, mixed rare and popular facts per batch) and the split results (Figure~\ref{fig:lr_duet_split}, each batch drawn from a single tier) show consistent forget and retain curves for \textbf{AdaPop}. Batches consisting entirely of popular facts (small $\beta_i$) produce broader, lower-magnitude per-token gradients; batches of rare facts (large $\beta_i$) produce sharper, higher-magnitude signals; mixed batches fall between these extremes. In all three cases the epoch-level dual-ascent controller observes the resulting retain-loss drift and adjusts $\alpha$ accordingly, so the retain quality is protected regardless of which popularity tier dominates a given batch. This robustness would not hold without the controller: in the fixed-$\alpha$ ablation (Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}), homogeneous popular-fact batches cause retain collapse at moderate learning rates precisely because the fixed penalty cannot compensate for the changed gradient scale.

\section{Per-Epoch Unlearning Dynamics}
\label{app:epoch_dynamics}

Figure~\ref{fig:epoch_dynamics} traces forget and retain ROUGE-L Recall across training epochs (0--10) on DUET for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct at $\text{lr}=10^{-4}$, covering seven baseline algorithms. AdaPop is not included: its per-epoch behaviour is characterised separately through the learning-rate sweep (Appendix~\ref{app:lr_sensitivity}), where the dual-ascent dynamics are the primary object of study.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{latex/img/epoch_analysis_all_colored_Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.jpeg}
    \caption{Forget (left) and retain (right) ROUGE-L Recall per training epoch on DUET, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, at $\text{lr}=10^{-4}$.}
    \label{fig:epoch_dynamics}

\paragraph{GA: immediate and irreversible collapse.}
GA reaches forget ROUGE-L $= 0.000$ and retain ROUGE-L $= 0.000$ after epoch~1 and remains at zero for all subsequent epochs. The unbounded ascent signal destroys both the target knowledge and general linguistic coherence in a single pass; no recovery mechanism exists.

\paragraph{GD: fast forgetting, slow retain recovery.}
GD achieves strong forgetting at epoch~1 (forget $= 0.012$) but retain collapses simultaneously to $0.345$. Over subsequent epochs the retain regularisation term gradually pulls the model back ($0.817 \to 0.692 \to 0.814 \to \ldots \to 0.932$ at epoch~10), but forget quality simultaneously drifts upward slightly ($0.012 \to 0.054 \to 0.025 \to \ldots \to 0.016$). The trajectory is unstable for the first several epochs and the final operating point at epoch~10 still degrades retain by roughly $0.035$ relative to Orig. Practitioners must choose an early stopping epoch carefully, as the best forget--retain balance is transient.

\paragraph{NPO: oscillating and slow forgetting.}
NPO's forget ROUGE-L is non-monotonic throughout training: $0.737 \to 0.728 \to 0.393 \to 0.621 \to 0.360 \to 0.423 \to 0.400 \to 0.196$ across epochs 1--10. Retain quality is stable ($\geq 0.924$) at all epochs, but the large oscillations in the forget curve make it difficult to determine a reliable stopping criterion. At epoch~10 forget is $0.196$, still well above the values achieved by WGA or GD. The oscillation pattern is consistent with the preference-alignment objective converging slowly for highly-memorised facts, as the KL penalty prevents sufficiently large updates.

\paragraph{PDU: crash-and-recover with stagnation.}
PDU collapses at epoch~1 (forget $= 0.000$, retain $= 0.001$), then partially recovers: by epoch~2 forget rises to $0.316$ and retain to $0.577$, continuing to $0.407/0.648$ at epoch~3. From epoch~4 onward forget stabilises near $0.41$ and retain near $0.97$--$0.98$, but the algorithm stagnates there and cannot push forget below $0.39$. The crash-and-recover trajectory is distinctive but the final forget quality is poor, comparable to NPO at its best epoch.

\paragraph{RMU and UNDIAL: no forgetting on entity-centric QA.}
RMU and UNDIAL are designed for skill-based unlearning (e.g., WMDP hazardous knowledge) and do not achieve meaningful forgetting on factual entity-centric QA. RMU forget ROUGE-L stays between $0.874$ and $0.914$ across all epochs; UNDIAL between $0.901$ and $0.920$. Both methods maintain near-perfect retain ($\geq 0.979$). Their failure on DUET is expected: the gradient re-routing mechanism in RMU and the decoupled update in UNDIAL target distributional suppression rather than fact-level parametric erasure.

\paragraph{WGA: fastest monotonic convergence.}
WGA is the strongest baseline in terms of training dynamics: forget ROUGE-L decreases monotonically from $0.122$ at epoch~1 to $0.020$ at epoch~10, while retain stays at $\geq 0.945$ throughout and reaches $1.000$ at epoch~10. The trajectory is smooth and does not require early stopping: any epoch beyond~4 (forget $= 0.042$) represents a viable operating point. These dynamics motivate the choice of WGA as the primary comparison baseline in Section~\ref{sec:comparison}.

\paragraph{Overall conclusions.}
The per-epoch dynamics reveal that stable, monotonic convergence is rare among the baselines: GA destroys the model instantly; GD and PDU exhibit transient collapse; NPO oscillates without converging. Only WGA consistently improves epoch-over-epoch on DUET without retain degradation. RMU and UNDIAL are inapplicable to this problem class. The dual-ascent controller in AdaPop is specifically designed to address the stability gap that the GD and NPO trajectories expose: by treating the retain constraint as a feedback signal rather than a fixed penalty, it prevents both the early collapse seen in GD and the oscillation seen in NPO.
