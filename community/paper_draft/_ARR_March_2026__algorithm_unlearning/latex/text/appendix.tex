\section{Derivation of AdaPop Popularity Parameters}

The power-law relationship $\beta_i = a \cdot s_i^{-b}$ is designed to map the wide dynamic range of popularity scores to a stable interval suitable for gradient updates. Since the gap between rare and popular entities typically spans 2--3 orders of magnitude in most popularity proxy metrics, the coefficients $a$ and $b$ must be tuned to ensure that unlearning remains effective yet controlled.

Specifically, we aim for "rare" entities (low $s_i$) to scale toward $\beta \approx 2.0$ to sharpen the update, while "popular" entities (high $s_i$) should scale toward $\beta \approx 0.05$ to flatten the update and overcome memory inertia.

\paragraph{Wikidata Proxy.} When the popularity proxy labeling is based on Wikidata (e.g., number of site links or claims), we empirically find the following coefficients to be optimal:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $a = 58.7$
    \item $b = 0.796$
\end{itemize}

These parameters may vary slightly depending on the specific range and distribution of the popularity proxy metric used (e.g., \texttt{pop\_sum} vs. \texttt{pagerank}).
